Our process / Working together

A development process built around the work.

We approach software development by first understanding the problem, the people who will use it, and the environment in which the software will or already does operate. Planning, implementation, testing, and delivery then follow the needs and scope of the project.

Working principle

Appropriate engineering, not unnecessary complexity.

Our belief is that the engineering approach should reflect the requirements and risks of the project. Planning, testing, and documentation are important, but the amount and form they take should make sense for the software being developed.

e.g. A one page business site and a web app storing medical information have very different needs.

01 / Project stages

Understanding, planning, building, and delivering.

These four stages describe a typical project. The details change with the size and type of work.

01

Understand the need

Begin by discussing what the client wants to accomplish, who will use the software, and what systems or processes are already involved. Identify constraints and clarify what a useful outcome would look like.

Typical result: A shared understanding of the problem, objectives, and relevant constraints.

02

Plan the work

Use what we learned in the initial discussion to identify the work that needs to be done. Agree on the expected deliverables, responsibilities, and important limitations before development begins.

Typical result: A defined set of deliverables, priorities, and project boundaries.

03

Develop and collaborate

Work through the agreed scope, developing and testing the software while reviewing progress with the client when appropriate. Feedback and technical findings help guide necessary adjustments without losing sight of the project's goals.

Typical result: Working software that has been tested and reviewed against the agreed requirements.

04

Finalize and deliver

Work with the client to review the final deliverables and coordinate their release or handoff. Depending on the project, this may involve deployment, documentation, knowledge transfer, or a transition to separately agreed maintenance.

Typical result: Agreed deliverables, confirmed handoff responsibilities, and a clear path for any follow-up work.

02 / Scope and expectations

Agree on the work before beginning.

An initial conversation helps establish what a project might involve, but it does not replace an agreed scope of work. Before development begins, we should understand the expected results, included features, responsibilities, and limitations.

New ideas and requirements can emerge during development. I welcome those discussions, but changes that affect the agreed work should be evaluated together before adjusting the scope, schedule, or cost.

03 / Different kinds of work

Different projects call for different approaches.

The same engineering principles apply, but how we plan, communicate, and deliver the work depends on what you need. Select the type of engagement that sounds closest to your situation.

Not sure which category fits? That's fine. Some projects involve more than one type of work, and we can discuss the appropriate approach together.

04 / Documentation and deliverables

Let's make things clear.

A software project involves more than the finished application. Depending on the work, you may receive planning documents, written agreements, technical documentation, working software, and information needed to operate or maintain it.
